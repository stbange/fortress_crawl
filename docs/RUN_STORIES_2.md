# Ten more runs, as stories

November 2026. Ten playtest-bot runs of 90 minutes in five new worlds: in each, one character
who follows the story (the quester) and one who goes as far out as it can (the delver). Every
line in quotation marks below is text the game showed them: chapter messages, the words of
dying enemies, and the inscriptions, notes and bounty boards they read on the way.

---

## Gerhold

### I. The Roll of Gerhold
*The quester, who wanted glory. Seed 2025.*

The Roll of the Dead hangs by the gate of Gerhold, and the first name on it was Edoeth
Nine-Fingers. The newcomer read the walls on the way down ("The water is bad. The bread is
bad. The dead are not.") and a bounty board offering 76 crowns for Aldimund the Unwashed. Four
minutes in, Edoeth was dead and the stonecutters carved a new name below his. Above it now
stood Varamund Ash-Tongue, and on the body were sealed orders under the wax of someone called
Ostaric.

Varamund lasted four more minutes. In his pack was a ledger: *"The gate of Gerhold was not
broken. It was opened from inside."* Only one name stood above the newcomer's now, and it was
Ostaric's. On the way to him the newcomer picked up the errands of the dead. A corpse with a
note asked for Mireslav's father's knife to be taken home; a charcoal scrawl begged for
Shendan's wooden horse, "back to the light." At minute 14 Ostaric fell, and said, dying: *"I
only opened the door. Ask Aumimar who walked through."* For one night the newcomer's name was
first on the Roll. By morning the singers had a new one, Coriwen the Grey.

The deeper it went, the less the walls wanted it there. *"Count the steps down. If the number
changes, turn back." "Three knocks below: close the door. Four: it is too late." "Tithe to the
First Masons: one warden each winter. Do not ask where they go."* Coriwen sent hunters twice.
The newcomer found the forge at home and became a passable smith. When Coriwen died at minute
41, the brand of the First Masons was burned into the corpse: *they are not gone.* Leofwen the
Thrice-Hanged came next, then Aumimar himself. *"The First Masons did not seal the Red Silence.
They fed it, every winter, for a thousand years."* Near the end, in a dead man's pocket, was a
letter never sent: *"Tell my brother I found Gerhold. Tell no one else."* Aumimar died at
minute 78 with the last word of the run: *"We kept it fed. Now it is hungry. Ediric wants it
hungry."*

Chapter 7, level 18, 860 kills, never once fallen. The Roll's newest name: Uliard the
Thrice-Hanged.

### II. The lid
*The delver, who wanted the Vault of King Ediric. Seed 2025.*

The same fortress, another hunger: the First Masons had taken their gold into the dark, and
Edoeth Nine-Fingers kept the first piece of the map. The delver never went to Edoeth. It went
out instead, past the bounty boards, killing whatever wore a name: Mirislav the Weeping,
Draiana the Hollow (and with her the legendary Dawnbite), Yarerin the Quiet and Mirrin
Nine-Fingers, one after the other, for Gloamsong and Lastfang. Three steps out, it learned what
the quester in the other run was dying to learn: *"The First Masons raised Gerhold over the Red
Silence. They called it a gate. It was a lid."* On a tomb nearby: *"Here lies Shenogor, who saw
the Red Silence and laughed. Once."*

It spent its gold on the forge and grew into a real smith, five levels' worth of greatswords
of crackling iron and silver. Then it walked into the fifth step out, a kilometre and a half
from the gate, where the worms come up under you and the walls burn. It died seven times in
twenty-five minutes. Each time it came home to its island and furnished it with money it had no
other use for: a statue of the founder, banners, a carpet, golden braziers, flower beds, koi,
lanterns in the trees, a golden throne. One of its daggers had woken after seventy-five kills;
out in the fifth step it found a better one, a legendary jade dagger of masterwork grade, level
45, and carried that to the end.

Edoeth Nine-Fingers, the first name on its map, never saw it.

## Halmark

### III. Silver nails
*The quester, who wanted glory. Seed 777.*

*"We sealed the door with silver nails. It was not enough."* That was the first thing the
newcomer read in Halmark, and it set the tone. By minute 4 Zlaoana the Last was dead and its
name was on the Roll. By minute 8 the next name, Shenerin Ash-Tongue, was dead too, and the
ledger said what ledgers in this world always say: *"The gate of Halmark was not broken. It was
opened from inside."* It took legendaries off the named as it went: Gloamreaver, Ironveil,
Starcall. Yarana, dying at minute 12: *"I only opened the door. Ask Ashusut who walked
through."*

Between the chapters, the dungeon passed it scraps. *"A torn page: '...Raarah the Thrice-Hanged
keeps the keys. Do not let it see you count...'"* Raiim Lantern-Jaw sent hunters, then died
with the First Masons' brand on him. Ishzad the Unburied's death brought the worst of it: *"The
First Masons did not seal the Pale Hunger. They fed it, every winter, for a thousand years."*
At minute 43, three steps out, the quester burned to death for the only time in the run. It
came back, and at 54 it stood over Ashusut: *"We kept it fed. Now it is hungry. Eyovik wants it
hungry."*

In the last half hour it went four steps out, nearly a kilometre from the gate. It learned the
forge, read *"We sealed the stair with our own dead. It was not enough,"* and remembered the
torn page: at minute 76 it found Raarah the Thrice-Hanged, the keeper of the keys, and killed
him for Moonwarden. The last note it read, at minute 87, belonged to someone called Ulmund:
*"I hear the dark that breathes. The others say there is nothing. The others are gone."*

Chapter 7, level 18, one death.

### IV. The keeper of the keys
*The delver, who wanted Uleard's Treasury. Seed 777.*

In the same Halmark, the map ran through Zlaoana the Last, and the delver ignored it. A boss
floating eye killed it in the first step out at minute 13. After that it went down carefully,
reading graves: *"Here lies Berawen, who saw the Pale Hunger and laughed. Once."* It killed
Berwen Ash-Tongue for Ironmourn. Then it found a torn page: *"...Corays Nine-Fingers keeps the
keys. Do not let it see you count..."* Six minutes later, Corays was dead. The delver took his
Bitterthorn, a staff that fights beside you when your health runs low, and carried it to the
end. *"Whoever reads this: the lamps here are lit by no one."* A second page named another
keeper, Caeloaine the Weeping, whom it never found. At minute 60 it went home and paid for a
statue of the founder and the banners of its house. Level 17, 654 kills, one death, and
Zlaoana still at large.

## Morburg

### V. The Black Psalter
*The quester, who wanted the truth. Seed 31415.*

What lies beneath Morburg? Varihold the Thrice-Hanged kept the first page of the Black Psalter
of Aldaeth, and getting to him cost the quester three deaths in three minutes. The page ended
mid-sentence; Aldeth of the Black Lantern had the next. On the walls: *"A list of names, all
crossed out. The last one is Gerios. Not crossed out yet."* And scrawled in blood: *"NOT DOWN.
NOT DOWN. NOT DOWN."* It died three more times to trolls, skeletons and a boss brute before
minute 9.

Aldeth's page completed a thought: *"Two pages, one truth. Edamund wrote the book."* With it was
the ledger: the gate had been opened from inside. The quester read on as it went: *"Edehold's
Shafts was dug for silver. They found the Ninth Dark instead." "Here lies Gerihold, who saw the
Ninth Dark and laughed. Once."* Boss brutes killed it twice more. At minute 44, Edamund: *"I
only opened the door. Ask Lindan who walked through."* The Psalter was complete, and it named
another book: the Book of Cedios, whose first page Radowei the Blind kept.

Radowei's page pointed to Bogorin the Flayed, and the corpse bore the brand of the Grey Abbots:
*they are not gone.* At minute 80 Bogorin's page closed the circle: *"Two pages, one truth.
Lindan wrote the book. The Grey Abbots did not seal the Ninth Dark. They fed it, every winter,
for a thousand years."*

Chapter 6, level 16, and nine deaths, the hardest-won story of the ten. It ended with a mace
it had used so long it had a name, Slimebane, after 231 kills.

### VI. Edwick
*The delver, who wanted revenge. Seed 31415.*

They burned Edwick. The delver's brother was in the chapel when they barred the door, and the
one who held the bar was Varihold the Thrice-Hanged. Then the delver went the wrong way for
revenge: outward. It killed Ingofrid the Blind for Frostveil and Eyagga the Weeping for
Hollowbrand, and it walked past doors that read *"Sealed by order of the Grey Abbots. Do not
open. Do not listen."* Three steps out, a kilometre from the gate, it found a phoenix feather,
and an inscription that the other Morburg run never saw: *"The Ninth Dark does not sleep. It
waits for the door."* It learned the forge (an axe, a silver mace, a silver greatsword,
smithing 4), lost twice, to an elite knight and a floating eye, and ended with a tempered sword
at level 34. Varihold is still alive, in this telling.

## Brangard

### VII. Half a map
*The quester, who wanted riches. Seed 1234.*

It began well. *"Pray quietly. Something below counts the prayers." "Do not dig below. -
Edieth, last of the wardens."* Within a minute it had killed Eyagga Ember-Eyed and taken a share
of the hoard and half a map; the other half was with Ingaulf Hookhand. Then it went into the
first step out and died four times in two minutes, to brutes, a troll and a hound. A dead man
called Skodis had left a note on the way: *"Three days without light. The walls are warm here.
Why are the walls warm?"* And everywhere, the same warning: *"Do not answer if it calls you by
name."*

Ingaulf Hookhand sent hunters every ten minutes, eight times in all, and was never found. The
quester killed Eyydis Nine-Fingers for Emberwake and read the burial customs of the place: *"The
dead of the Last Siege are buried facing down. So they cannot find the way up." "The Drowned
Host buried Vyorn here. Standing up. In case."* For the last hour it wandered the first step out
and did not get far. Chapter 2, level 11. Half a map.

### VIII. Two kilometres
*The delver, who wanted the truth. Seed 1234.*

The other half of Brangard went farther than anyone in either batch: the fifth step out, two
kilometres from the gate. It read the history as it went: *"In the Last Siege the bells rang for
nine days. No one knows who rang them." "The Quiet Year: the night the gate stood open. Who
opened it?"* And a diary: *"Came for gold. Found the Weeping Kin. Should have stayed a
scribe."* It killed Eyaulf Ember-Eyed for Hollowmaw, Ingadis the Unwashed for Ashkeeper and
Hrodis Saltmouth for Moonbrand.

Then, at minute 57, an elite worm came up under it four steps out, and it began to die: nine
times in thirty minutes, to worms, brutes, skeletons and fire. Between deaths it went home and
spent like someone settling their affairs. At minute 59 it bought eight decorations in one go,
statue, banners, carpet, braziers, flowers, koi, lanterns, a golden throne; later a crystal
chandelier, a sundial and an orrery. Its rime-iron dagger woke after seventy-five kills, a
masterwork; a sealed weapon from the smith turned out to be Emberwake, a legendary of this
world. *"Scrawled in blood: 'NOT DOWN. NOT DOWN. NOT DOWN.'"* It ended at level 20 with a
greatsword of level 68 found out there. Eyagga Ember-Eyed, who kept the first page of the Black
Psalter of Braniric, sent hunters after it every ten minutes all the way out; the truth it had
come for was never opened.

## Varkeep

### IX. The list
*The quester, who wanted the truth. Seed 4711.*

Ishisut the Thrice-Hanged kept the first page of the Book of Vararic. On the way to him the
quester kept passing the same wall: *"A list of names, all crossed out. The last one is
Ostewyn. Not crossed out yet."* It died five times before it reached him at minute 24. The page
pointed to Ilsut the Unburied. *"Here the Thorned Hand hanged a bellringer for listening at the
stair."* A diary: *"Came for gold. Found the Thorned Hand. Should have stayed a scribe."* Then
the bot got lost for half an hour, and the last part of the run is a loop of the same
corridors: *"Sealed by order of the Lantern Kings. Do not open. Do not listen."* A shield:
*"Held Ashiim's Cells for the Ninefold Wardens. Paid in full."* Ilsut sent hunters four times.
Chapter 2, level 11.

### X. Wet clay
*The delver, who wanted revenge. Seed 4711.*

They burned Halham; its brother was in the chapel; Ishisut held the bar. The delver fought 729
monsters in ninety minutes and did not meet a single one with a name. What it met was writing.
*"I hear the footsteps that match mine. The others say there is nothing. The others are gone."*
*"We sealed the lower gate with wet clay. It was not enough."* And at minute 38, the same truth
every world seems to keep under its gate: *"The Lantern Kings raised Varkeep over the Under-Sun.
They called it a gate. It was a lid."* It opened one vault's hoard, bought a statue of the
founder, and never died. Level 16, three steps out, a quiet run.

## What these ten runs show

- **The story carries when it is followed.** Two questers reached chapter 7 and one chapter 6.
  The chain of the Roll and the chain of the books tell a real mystery: the gate opened from
  inside, the traitor who only opened the door, the builders who fed what lies below, and the
  one who wants it hungry. The inscriptions underline it beautifully, and they sometimes pay off.
  The torn page naming Raarah, the keeper of the keys, led one quester straight to him.
- **But every world tells the same mystery.** The beats are identical from world to world (the
  ledger, "I only opened the door", "they fed it every winter", "now it is hungry"), with only
  the names changed. In one world it is fresh; in the second it is a template. The next step for
  the story would be more than one shape of metaplot.
- **Two questers stalled at chapter 2** with 70 minutes left: their second target was never
  found, only its hunters, eight times. The hunters work as a reminder, but they do not yet
  bring the target closer.
- **Delvers die far out.** Two died 7 and 9 times in the fourth and fifth steps out (worms,
  burning, elite brutes), while questers near the gate died early to boss brutes. The middle
  game is gentle; the edges are not.
- **Text fixed after this batch:** the lost-item note read *"Dropped my Mireslav's father's
  knife - no. Mireslav's father's knife."*; it now reads *"Beside Mireslav, a last note:
  'Mireslav's father's knife. Lost it ...'"*. Sentences and quotations that began with "the"
  now start with a capital, and *"the Pale Wardens remembers. the Pale Wardens does not
  forgive"* became *"Remembered by the Pale Wardens. Not forgiven."*
