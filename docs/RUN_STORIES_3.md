# Ten runs in ten generated worlds

November 2026, after the generated stories ([STORY_GEN.md](STORY_GEN.md)). Ten quester bots,
90 minutes each, in ten new worlds, so ten different histories. Taken from the bots' logs,
which record every story message, every question answered and every hunters' order. Each
world's hidden truth is from `worldmap history`.

Ten worlds, ten different truths: who opened the seal (a scribe, a grieving keeper, the heir
of a bargain's line, an exile's sister...), whom they blamed, and what is below.

## Edhold (seed 1337): the glory of the Roll

The quester came for glory. The first name on the Roll of the Dead was Tamumun, captain of the
Grey Abbots; at minute 4 it killed him, and on his body found the Abbots' orders: *"Gereos the
traitor opened the Salt Door. Let no one say otherwise."* The next name was Ishusut, carved on
the Roll for walling Gereos in alive. At minute 11 Ishusut fell. Then the Steward, Tamusut,
whose last words cracked the story: *"I named Gereos. But Gereos's key was gone the day before.
Gereos told me so. I did not listen."* The banner said **A QUESTION ANSWERED: DID GEREOS OPEN
IT, AS THE GREY ABBOTS SAY?**

At minute 18, a note in the dark answered the next one: Thyvik turned the Bone Key in the Salt
Door. The second arc confirmed it piece by piece: the Abbots' chronicle was forged; Gerahold,
dying, said Gereos and Thyvik were one blood, brother and sister; Ulawyn, Gereos's child,
dying: *"Thyvik opened the Salt Door with the Bone Key."* The Roll changed meaning at the turn:
*"Read down the Roll: it is not a roll of heroes. It is the list of everyone who kept the
secret of the Night of the Open Gate."* By minute 34 it knew why (Thyvik opened it so the world
would see the bones), and at 47 what was below: *"The knocking below stopped after three days.
Then they told of the Thing That Counts."* Its last target, Skyvik, sent hunters whose leader
carried Skyvik's orders from Svavik, the hand of Thyvik. Level 18, six chapters, five deaths,
the third step out.

## Aldburg (seed 8080): revenge for Cedstead

*They burned Cedstead. Your son was in the chapel when they barred the door.* Halewyn, who
held the bar, died at minute 3 with orders in Braneth's hand, and the Weeping Brotherhood's
version of the night: Edieth opened the seal. Cedowyn took 44 minutes to find. *"Ask
Braneth,"* he said, and died. At minute 58 the leader of a band of hunters carried the answer
to why: *Cedstead burned because the child of Edieth hid there, and Braneth wanted no one left
to ask questions.* At minute 67 Braneth died saying exactly that, and that he had named Edieth
though her key had been stolen the day before. Two questions answered; the next target,
Ysewen, "knows who gave the orders, and why". Level 15, one death.

## Halmark (seed 777): the Roll again, another lie

The Pale Covenant's orders said Ulfern opened the seal; Olerin, carved on the Roll for hanging
Ulfern at the gate, admitted dying that Ulfern's key was gone the day before. Three chapters in
14 minutes, then the bot got stuck in the second arc for the rest of the run.

## Gerhold, Ulmark, Ulwall (tragedies): the truth from the walls

Three quester bots that got stuck after the first chapter still learned the deepest secrets
from what they read. In **Gerhold** a note answered *What lies beneath Gerhold?* at minute 40
(*"The Red Silence is a name the First Masons gave to the people they walled in"*) and at
minute 57 *why the seal was opened*: Ulwen, a keeper, had lost a daughter to the dark below the
seal and heard her voice among the ones below, singing. In **Ulwall** the very first stone near
the gate, at minute 3, said the Sleeper is no monster but Branoos, the king who was there first.
In **Ulmark** the truth quester read a page of Ostoeth's Ledger, the account the Silent Watch
tore up: Ulhold chained the last king under the mountain and called him the Pale Hunger.

## The rest

In **Aldgard**, **Gerburg** and **Varmark** (riches) and **Cedmark** (glory) the bots took the first
target or none, then lost themselves in the dungeon; their stories were carried only by the
hunters, who came every ten minutes with orders naming whom their master answered to.

## What the ten runs show

- **Every world told a different story.** Three human dramas with a forged chronicle and a
  scapegoat (in two the opener's own sibling), three tragedies about people walled in and
  called monsters, a bargain falling due, a hunger fed with children. No two openers had the
  same reason.
- **The lie and its crack land early.** Every quester that reached the third chapter got the
  liars' orders, the punishment and the dying admission, and the banner answering *was it
  them?* That arc works as a first act.
- **Clues reach players who do not follow the chapters.** Questions were answered from notes,
  stones, pages and hunters' orders by bots stuck on their first target. One stone near the
  gate gave away what lies below at minute 3: the founding's secret, as an old fact, had been
  placed by the gate. Fixed: it now lies no nearer than about 300 m, and key facts no
  revelation carries no nearer than about 200 m.
- **The answer to "why" names the opener**, so it now also answers "who".
- **The Roll of the Dead spoiled the turn once:** a target without a deed of its own borrowed a
  revelation for its legendary's lore, and the Roll showed it a chapter early. Fixed: such a
  target's line on the Roll is now its title.
- **The bots still get stuck** in seven of ten runs, which caps the chapters; that says more
  about the bot than the stories.
